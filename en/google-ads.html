Google Ads native tag: conversion measurement

In 230 seconds — what you will find here

  1. What the Google Ads native tag is and how it relates to GA4.
  2. Why Conversion Linker, click identifiers and consent must be designed together.
  3. How to pass value, currency and order ID without duplicating conversions.
  4. When it makes sense to consider enhanced conversions and which checks come first.
  5. A practical implementation sequence in Google Tag Manager and five operational FAQs.

The official reference is the Google tag guide.

What the Google Ads native tag is

The Google Ads native tag is the conversion tag that measures, directly in the advertising account, the actions you have configured as conversions: a purchase, a form submission, a call, a click on WhatsApp. It is not a report and it is not a CMS plugin. It is a script, usually published through Google Tag Manager, that fires only when the action has actually happened and sends Google Ads the parameters defined for that action.

It is called “native” to distinguish it from importing conversions from Google Analytics 4. In the first case the event is born for Ads. In the second case the event is born in GA4 and is then linked to the advertising account. They are two legitimate paths, with different windows, attribution models and processing times. Choosing one, or using them together without a plan, changes how campaigns read the results.

A serious implementation is not just pasting a snippet. You need a conversion action defined in the account, a reliable trigger on the site, the right parameters (value, currency, identifier) and a check that the same action is not counted twice by overlapping setups.

Why it matters for campaigns

Google Ads automated strategies use conversions as a signal. If the signal is late, incomplete or doubled, the account optimizes against a wrong picture of the business. It is not a detail to “fix later”: it is the basis for deciding whether a campaign is working.

The native tag is often useful when the action to optimize is close to advertising and must be observed with the Ads account settings: primary or secondary action, conversion window, count “one” or “every”, fixed or dynamic value. GA4 remains the tool for understanding journeys. Ads is the tool for telling campaigns which outcome counts.

There is also a timing issue. Processing can vary between platforms and conversion types. Before comparing an Ads report with a GA4 report on the same day, check diagnostics, action status and windows. Two different numbers do not automatically mean an error: they can describe different rules applied to the same clicks.

  • Primary action: it is the one bidding strategies can use. Keep it few and clean.
  • Count: a lead is usually counted once; a purchase often every time, with an order identifier.
  • Value: if the margin changes from order to order, a fixed value flattens the signal.

Conversion Linker and click identifiers

The Conversion Linker helps store information about the advertising interaction within the limits allowed by the browser and the consent settings. Click parameters can include identifiers such as gclid, and on some paths wbraid or gbraid. Without this link, a conversion that happens hours after the click can arrive “orphaned” and not be attributed to the right campaign.

The linker must be published on every page of the site, not only on the thank-you page, and must be aligned with consent. If the user has not accepted advertising measurement, it makes no sense to force cookies or identifiers. Configure the tag according to Google’s current guidance, check the domains involved and, if the path crosses several domains (site, external checkout, subdomain), check that the link does not break halfway.

In Google Tag Manager the Conversion Linker is a dedicated tag, firing on all pages and respecting consent. It does not replace the conversion tag: it prepares it. The two pieces work in sequence.

Conversion actions and dynamic values

Every useful conversion must have a clear name in the account and an equally clear trigger on the site. “All clicks on the Submit button” is fragile: the button changes class, the form is reloaded, a user clicks twice. Better a DataLayer event that the site publishes only when the action is confirmed, for example generate_lead after the server response or purchase on the completed-order page.

For an ecommerce store the fields that make the difference are few and must be read from the DataLayer, not guessed from the page text:

  • Value: an amount consistent with the chosen criterion (order total, net of VAT or of shipping: decide it once and do not change it mid-year).
  • Currency: ISO code, for example EUR.
  • Transaction ID: unique order identifier, useful to limit double counting if the thank-you page is reloaded.
  • Items: when the setup requires them, a product list with id, name, price and quantity.

An example of a message the tag can read, after the developer has published it on a confirmed order:

dataLayer.push({
  event: 'purchase',
  ecommerce: {
    transaction_id: 'ORD-2026-98451',
    value: 249.00,
    currency: 'EUR'
  }
});

The conversion tag must not fire on the simple load of every page. It fires on the event. In GTM you create a data layer variable for ecommerce.value, ecommerce.currency and ecommerce.transaction_id, and map them to the Ads tag fields.

Enhanced conversions, with the right checks

Enhanced conversions use user-provided data — usually an email or a phone number left in the form or at checkout — normalized and hashed according to Google’s specifications. They recover conversions that the browser, on its own, would no longer be able to connect to the click, within the technical and consent limits of the moment.

They are not a switch to turn on “because everyone does it”. Before sending any personal data, check four things: that the conversion action supports the feature, that the legal basis and the site privacy notice allow it, that advertising consent is respected, and that the fields arrive clean (lowercase, no spaces, consistent phone format). A badly written email, or one sent without consent, does not improve campaigns: it creates a compliance problem.

In GTM the data is read from dedicated variables; it is not written in clear text in container comments. Document which fields you use and on which forms. If a form does not collect an email, do not invent a value.

Native tag or import from GA4

Google Ads and GA4 have different purposes. GA4 describes behavior. Ads optimizes spend. Importing a conversion from GA4 is convenient when the event is already solid and you want a single definition. The native tag is preferable when you need direct control over the action used for bidding, over values and over the order identifier, without going through the GA4 model.

The most common mistake is having both as primary actions on the same sale. Campaigns see two conversions for a single order, cost per acquisition is cut in half on paper and bids shift. Choose one primary source for each goal and keep the other, if you need it, as a secondary control.

Before deciding, check in the account: which actions are primary, the conversion window, the attribution model and whether imports from GA4 or from another container are already active. There is no universal rule that always makes one of the two paths better.

How to implement it in Google Tag Manager

The sequence we use in EasyTag Pro projects is repeatable. First the measurement plan is settled, then the container. If the conversion is a click already on the page, start from Tracking Click Telefono, TAG Email or Contatti 3-in-1. For product values and feeds, Tracker E-commerce and Dynamic Remarketing.

  1. In the Google Ads account create (or review) the action: name, category, value, count, window, inclusion in primary actions.
  2. On the site, have the DataLayer publish an event only when the conversion is confirmed, with the required fields.
  3. In GTM publish the Conversion Linker on all pages, subject to consent.
  4. Create the Google Ads conversion tag with the action ID and label, dynamic variables and a trigger on the event, not on “all pages”.
  5. If you use enhanced conversions, add only the fields that have been checked and documented.
  6. Preview: one fire per order, parameters present, no second twin tag.

After publishing, check the action diagnostics in Google Ads and Tag Assistant. The first data can take time to appear in reports: tag diagnostics are the fastest signal that the hit was sent.

Verification and maintenance

  • Check that the thank-you page, when reloaded, does not generate a second conversion: the order identifier is there for this.
  • Align consent and the linker. A tag that fires before the user’s choice pollutes both privacy and the numbers.
  • When you change theme, checkout or form, run Preview again. The DataLayer protects you from visual changes only if the events stay the ones you agreed.
  • Keep a log: which action is primary, where the value comes from, who published the last version of the container.

FAQ about the Google Ads tag

Does the conversion tag replace Google Analytics 4?

No. The Ads tag tells campaigns which action happened. GA4 is for analyzing journeys. They can coexist, as long as you do not count the same action twice as a primary conversion.

Do I have to put the tag on every page?

The Conversion Linker yes, across the whole site, respecting consent. The conversion tag no: only when the action is confirmed, through a precise trigger.

Why do Ads and GA4 not show the same number?

Because they apply different models, windows and filters, and because one can include conversions the other does not import. Compare the definitions before concluding that a tag is broken.

Are enhanced conversions mandatory?

No. They are a useful option in some accounts, after you have checked consent, the privacy notice and data quality. Without those checks it is better not to turn them on.

Who should set it up?

Marketing and development together: the first chooses the action and the value, the second publishes the event in the DataLayer, whoever manages GTM connects tags and triggers. EasyTag Pro does this join when the company does not have a person dedicated only to tracking.

Google Ads conversions and remarketing

What is the difference between a Google Ads conversion event and a remarketing event?

A conversion records a valuable action (purchase, lead, call, signup) and feeds reports and bidding, for example Target CPA or Target ROAS. Remarketing collects browsing or product signals to build audiences. The catalog case is Dynamic Remarketing.

How do you send a Google Ads conversion with gtag.js?

With gtag('event', 'conversion', { send_to: 'AW-CONVERSION_ID/CONVERSION_LABEL', value: 0, currency: 'EUR', transaction_id: '...' }). In GTM the same send is done with the native tag, not with custom HTML.

What is the send_to parameter for?

It indicates the AW-... account and the conversion label the action should be attributed to. Without a precise send_to, a site with several accounts sends the signal to the wrong destination.

When should you use transaction_id in Google Ads conversions too?

On purchases and on every action the user can repeat by reloading the page. The same id stops the thank-you page from counting two conversions.

How do enhanced conversions work and how do you pass user data?

They add user-provided data (email, phone, name, address) to the conversion tag, processed according to Google’s specifications, to recover conversions the cookie alone does not see. They must not be placed in clear text in GA4 parameters. Advertising consent is covered in Consent Mode.

How do you send user data in server-side GTM for Google Ads?

With a User-provided data variable linked to the Google tag, and with the Google Ads User-provided Data Event tag in the server container, using the correct Conversion ID.

What is dynamic remarketing and how does it differ from standard remarketing?

Standard remarketing uses lists of pages visited. Dynamic remarketing sends catalog parameters (value, currency, items with id and google_business_vertical) to show the items viewed or abandoned. That is the scope of Dynamic Remarketing.

Which parameters does retail dynamic remarketing ask for?

  • send_to toward the correct Ads destination.
  • value and currency.
  • items, and in each element an id identical to the Merchant Center product id.
  • google_business_vertical, for retail usually retail.

What happens if the product id does not match the Merchant Center feed?

Google Ads does not match the user to the catalog item. The dynamic ad is not personalized, or it uses a generic fallback.

Why use native Google Ads tag templates in GTM and not a custom HTML tag?

Native templates format the parameters, talk to the Conversion Linker, respect Consent Mode and stay aligned with the latest Ads features. Custom HTML reinvents those things, and usually breaks them.

Value, deduplication and advertising consent

How do you avoid a double conversion if the site has both gtag.js and GTM?

Keep a single system, preferably GTM with native tags, and remove the other send of the same event on the same trigger. Two tags on the same thank-you page count two conversions.

How do you track actions that do not reload the page, such as an AJAX form or a phone click?

The trigger must listen for a dataLayer event fired only after confirmation: for example form_submitted or phone_click. The container already ready for the phone number is Tracking Click Telefono; for phone, email and WhatsApp together, Contatti 3-in-1.

What are value and currency for in Google Ads?

They measure the economic return of the conversion and feed value-based strategies: Maximize conversion value and Target ROAS. Without a currency, the value is not comparable.

How do you tell variable values from fixed values?

For ecommerce and quotes the calculated value sits in the dataLayer, transaction by transaction: that is the Tracker E-commerce case. For a lead with a constant value you set a fixed amount on the Ads conversion, and you repeat it identically.

What is the difference between importing a conversion from GA4 and using the Google Ads native tag?

The native tag sends the signal directly to Google Ads, with Conversion Linker and enhanced conversions. The GA4 import depends on the key event and on the link between the two products, and it is less suitable when you need the first-party advertising click. The native tag model is the subject of this guide.

How do you check that a Google Ads conversion fires without errors?

In GTM Preview or in Tag Assistant you check triggers, variables and the consent state. In Network you look for requests to Google advertising domains, not only the tag name in green.

What happens if the conversion tag fires before the variables are ready?

value or transaction_id go out empty. Value, currency and id must be in the same dataLayer.push object that emits the event, or already be in the state before that push. The queue is explained in the DataLayer guide.

How do you handle consent on conversion and remarketing tags?

With Consent Mode: ad_storage, ad_user_data for user data used in advertising, ad_personalization for remarketing and personalized ads. The detail of the default and update commands is in the Consent Mode guide. The package is Consent Mode v2.

Can you send an event to only one Google Ads account?

Yes. send_to on the tag or on the event limits the destination to that account and that label. The other accounts linked on the page do not receive that conversion.

What is the relationship between the Google tag on all pages and conversion accuracy?

A tag present on the whole site keeps first-party cookies and identifiers along the session. Without it, the Conversion Linker does not see the click on the landing page and attribution gets shorter.

Conversion Linker and first-party cookies

What is the Conversion Linker and what is it for?

It stores advertising click data, such as the gclid, in a first-party cookie, so the conversion tag can find it when the action happens, even pages later.

Why is the Conversion Linker essential with third-party cookie limits?

Browsers block or shorten third-party cookies. Saving the click on the site domain makes the conversion readable without that third-party cookie.

On which pages should the Conversion Linker fire in GTM?

On all of them, with an All Pages trigger. Any landing page that receives an advertising click must be able to save the parameter immediately.

How does the Conversion Linker work in server-side tagging?

In the server container the Conversion Linker tag, usually on All Pages, writes and reads first-party cookies tied to the tagging server domain.

What is the difference between first-party and third-party cookies?

A first-party cookie is set by the domain of the page you are visiting. A third-party cookie is set by a domain different from the page domain.

Why do first-party cookies hold up better for measurement today?

They belong to the site being visited and escape the general block on third-party cookies. They remain subject to consent and to the expiry limits imposed by the browser.

How can browsers and servers still limit first-party cookies?

With ITP and duration caps, with wrong attributes (Domain, Path, SameSite, Secure) and with consent denied on analytics_storage or ad_storage. See Consent Mode.

What is the link between first-party cookies, analytics_storage and the GA4 client id?

If analytics_storage is granted, GA4 reads and writes first-party cookies to recognize user and session. If it is denied, it does not use that storage to identify them. Events guide: Google Analytics 4.

What is the link between first-party cookies, ad_storage and Conversion Linker?

The linker and advertising tags keep the click in first-party storage. If ad_storage is denied, that write is limited and the conversion cannot rely on the advertising cookie.

In what sense does the Google tag still measure when cookies are limited?

It combines first-party signals, linker parameters in the URL, Consent Mode and modeling. It is not a way to ignore a refusal: it is continuity of measurement inside the consent state.

Cross-domain, redirects and checklist

What is cross-domain measurement and when is it needed?

It is needed when the journey crosses two distinct domains, for example the site and an external checkout, and you want the same session instead of a new user or a self-referral.

What is the difference between subdomains and different domains?

www.example.com and shop.example.com share the parent domain and, if the cookie is set on .example.com, the first-party cookies too. domain-a.com and domain-b.com do not: link decoration is required.

How does the Google tag keep identity from domain-a.com to domain-b.com?

It decorates outgoing links and forms with a _gl parameter. The destination domain reads it and saves the state in its own first-party cookies.

Which mistakes make cross-domain fail?

  • A domain is missing from the configuration list.
  • A server-side redirect drops the query, and therefore _gl too.
  • A script intercepts the click and navigates without letting the link be decorated.

How do you check the handoff between AMP pages and non-AMP pages?

In an incognito window, or after clearing cookies, you check that cid and any gclid travel in the _gl parameter and match on the final page. Redirects in the middle are the first point that breaks.

How do 301 and 302 redirects affect the Conversion Linker and cross-domain?

If the redirect does not keep the query string, gclid and _gl are lost. The chain must be tested on the real arrival URL, not only on the original link.

How do you inspect cookies during debugging?

In Developer Tools: the Application tab, the Cookie list, and the Network tab to see domain, expiry and request payloads.

What is the checklist for robust tagging across dataLayer, GA4 and Google Ads?

  • Initialize window.dataLayer once, at the top, without reassigning it. Guide: DataLayer.
  • Events with typed parameters, numbers that are really numbers. Guide: GA4.
  • Native tags, not custom HTML, for Ads and Analytics.
  • Consent Mode with default before the tags and update from the CMP. Guide: Consent Mode.
  • Conversion Linker on all pages.
  • A cross-domain test on flows that change domain.
  • A final check in Tag Assistant and DebugView.

If internal time is missing, the scope is the Shop and the Supporto 5 ore, 10 ore or 20 ore packages.
